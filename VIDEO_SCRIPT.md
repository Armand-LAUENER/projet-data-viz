# Demo video script (~3 min 30)

Target 3:30 to stay inside the 2–4 min window. Written in short sentences, read
not improvised. **Show interactions live, don't just describe them.** English.

| Time | Screen | What to say (read) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Title + KPI header | "You've heard France is Europe's tax champion. In 2024 it raised 45.3 % of GDP in taxes and social contributions — about 5 points above the EU average. Let's see what that number really hides." |
| 0:20–0:40 | Open the Definition box | "This is the tax-to-GDP ratio from Eurostat: all taxes plus net social contributions, over GDP. One source, one definition, for every country — so the comparison is fair. It is not a personal tax rate." |
| 0:40–1:20 | Map + ranking; move the year slider | "France sits second, just behind Denmark and ahead of Belgium — exactly Eurostat's official top three. Watch the ranking as I move the year slider: the top of the table barely changes. This is not new." |
| 1:20–2:00 | Lines + gap chart; add/remove a country | "Over thirty years the French line stays well above the EU average. The gap chart makes it explicit — it hovers around plus five points the whole time. Note the shaded crisis years: ratios can jump just because GDP falls, not because taxes rose." |
| 2:00–2:45 | Composition: stacked bars + table; change compared countries | "Now the key part — what is the gap made of? Two blocs, roughly equal: production taxes, plus two-point-eight points, and social contributions, plus two-point-six. But income and wealth taxes? France is actually *below* the EU average. So it's about how social spending is financed, not higher tax on everything." |
| 2:45–3:10 | Data-quality section | "I don't ask you to take my word for it. Zero duplicates, categories that reconcile to the total, and the pipeline reproduces the official France and EU figures. Where my snapshot differs — EU 40.2 vs 40.4 — it's a documented revision, shown openly." |
| 3:10–3:30 | Conclusions | "So: France really is near the top, the gap is structural, and it comes from how social spending is funded. What the data *cannot* say: nothing causal, nothing about whether that money is well spent. Thanks for watching." |

**Checklist before recording:** app awake (not hibernating); default filters
reset; private-window test of the link; screen at a readable zoom.
