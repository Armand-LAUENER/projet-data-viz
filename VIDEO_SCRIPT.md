# Demo video script (~3 min 30)

Target 3:30 inside the 2–4 min window. Short sentences, read not improvised.
**Show interactions live.** English. Sober tone (the topic involves real deaths).

| Time | Screen | What to say (read) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Title + KPI | "In 2024, 3 193 people were killed on French metropolitan roads — about nine every day. But a death is not random. The question: *when, where and for whom* does an accident turn deadly?" |
| 0:20–0:40 | Definitions box | "The data is the BAAC — a police form for every injury accident, from the ONISR. My key metric is the gravity index: deaths per 100 accidents. One source, no external denominator." |
| 0:40–1:15 | Heatmap, toggle metric | "Accidents peak at rush hour — watch when I switch to the gravity index: the map lights up at night and in the weekend small hours. A night accident is nearly twice as deadly." |
| 1:15–1:55 | Map + share bars | "Now the heart of it. Switch the map to gravity: it *inverts*. Paris has the most accidents but the lowest death rate; rural Dordogne and Jura, the opposite. Only 37 % of accidents are outside town — but 68 % of deaths are." |
| 1:55–2:35 | Who: dumbbell + pyramid, filter a mode | "Who dies? Powered two-wheelers: 22 % of deaths, far above their share of traffic. E-scooters the opposite — many injuries, few deaths. And two age peaks: the 18–24 and the 75+." |
| 2:35–3:05 | Quality + ethics | "I don't ask you to trust me. The pipeline reproduces the official ONISR count exactly — 3 193. Zero duplicates. I show what's fragile: missing data on hit-and-run offenders from 2021. And only aggregates are deployed — no individual victim is ever exposed." |
| 3:05–3:30 | Conclusions | "So: frequency and severity tell opposite stories; night multiplies the danger; and the toll falls on specific modes and ages. What I *can't* say: no per-kilometre risk, no causation. Thanks for watching." |

**Before recording:** app awake (not hibernating); filters at defaults; private-window
link test; readable zoom.
